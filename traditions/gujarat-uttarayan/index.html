Gujarat’s Uttarayan

In Gujarat, Makar Sankranti is Uttarayan, a public kite festival around 14 January. The foods repeatedly named with it are undhiyu, chikki, and jalebi.

The festival

The English Wikipedia entry for the International Kite Festival in Gujarat describes Uttarayan as the January kite festival marking the sun’s move into Capricorn, the end of winter, and the start of the harvest. It says the festival is a two-day public holiday in Gujarat, and that the second day is called Vasi Uttarayan. Ahmedabad has hosted an international kite festival as part of the official celebration. Mint dates that official festival to 1989.

People fly kites from rooftops and open grounds. That is the part this site can verify from those write-ups. It is not a claim about every household in the state.

The table

Wikipedia lists undhiyu, chikki, and jalebi as foods commonly prepared for the festival. The Times of India has called undhiyu with puri, plus jalebi, the flavor of Uttarayan in Ahmedabad. Mint describes undhiyu as the Surat mixed-vegetable dish whose name comes from the Gujarati word for upside down, traditionally cooked in a sealed earthen pot set over a pit, and now often made in a pot on the stove. Mint also names chikki, a peanut or sesame brittle with jaggery, and jalebi.

Other dishes show up in festival reporting, including kachariyu and khichdo. This page stays with the three that the festival summaries agree on. The undhiyu and chikki recipes on KiteKitchen are home adaptations, not the buried-pot original and not a restaurant formula.

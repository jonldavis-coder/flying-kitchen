Flying BBQ · Plane

Asian grills by plane

Cumin lamb, char siu, galbi, yakitori, gai yang, satay, and lechón.

This is a long flight on purpose. Xinjiang lamb skewers, a Beijing duck, Cantonese char siu, Korean galbi, yakitori, Isaan grilled chicken, Malaysian satay, and a Cebu lechón do not belong to one cuisine.

The plane is just how a reader covers that distance. Each stop keeps its own wood, sugar, and rule for doneness.

Stops

  1. Xinjiang Lamb Skewers · China — Lamb skewers with salt, cumin, and dried chili, grilled over a street fire.
  2. Home-Roast Peking Duck · China — Roast duck with lacquered skin, eaten with scallion, cucumber, and wheat pancakes.
  3. Cantonese Char Siu · China — Sweet-salty pork with a lacquered edge, five spice, and a little char.
  4. Korean Beef Galbi · South Korea — Beef short ribs in a sweet soy marinade with pear, garlic, and sesame.
  5. Japanese Yakitori · Japan — Chicken thigh on small skewers, salted, with a clean charcoal taste.
  6. Thai Gai Yang · Thailand — Grilled chicken with garlic, coriander root, white pepper, and fish sauce.
  7. Malaysian Chicken Satay · Malaysia — Turmeric chicken skewers with a warm peanut sauce.
  8. Cebu Lechon · Philippines — Slow-roasted pork with salt, garlic, lemongrass, and a crackled skin if the heat allows it.

Caribbean by boat

Mojo, jerk, and island pigs, harbor to harbor.

These fires moved by sea. Sour orange and garlic in Cuba, allspice and scotch bonnet in Jamaica, a boucan in Haiti, wasakaka in the Dominican Republic, lechón in Puerto Rico, chadon beni in Trinidad, flying fish in Barbados.

The boat is the vehicle because that is how pigs, chile, and citrus actually traveled. Each stop is its own kitchen.

Stops

  1. Cuban Lechón Asado · Cuba — Skin-on pork with garlic and sour orange, roasted until the skin crackles.
  2. Jamaican Jerk Chicken · Jamaica — Chicken with allspice, thyme, and Scotch bonnet, smoky if the fire has pimento wood.
  3. Haitian Boukannen · Haiti — Grilled pork with garlic, thyme, and a hot pepper, edged with smoke.
  4. Dominican Wasakaka Chicken · Dominican Republic — Roasted or grilled chicken with a fresh garlic and herb sauce.
  5. Puerto Rican Lechón Asado · Puerto Rico — Roast pork seasoned with garlic, oregano, and achiote, with a salted skin.
  6. Trinidadian Grilled Kingfish · Trinidad and Tobago — Grilled kingfish or mahi with culantro, garlic, and a hot pepper.
  7. Barbadian Grilled Flying Fish · Barbados — A light, herb-seasoned fish with lime, whether it is flying fish or a named substitute.

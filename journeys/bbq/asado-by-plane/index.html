Asado and churrasco by plane

A flight across the southern fires, from the parrilla to picanha.

The distance from a Buenos Aires parrilla to a Brazilian picanha skewer is a plane ride, not a stroll. The fire idea is shared. The cuts are not.

This route starts with an Argentine asado, crosses the Río de la Plata, looks south to a Patagonian lamb, steps into a Chilean asado, and ends on Brazilian picanha. It is not a ranking of who grills better.

Stops

  1. Argentine Asado · Argentina — Salted beef cooked over wood coals, with chimichurri as a table sauce.
  2. Uruguayan Asado al Espeto · Uruguay — Beef roasted slowly beside wood, on a cross rather than a quick grate.
  3. Patagonian Cordero al Asador · Argentina — Lamb roasted beside a wood fire, salted, with a browned outside.
  4. Chilean Asado · Chile — Salted beef from a Chilean grill, with a little merkén and a fresh relish beside it.
  5. Brazilian Picanha · Brazil — Rump cap grilled with its fat cap on, seasoned only with coarse salt.

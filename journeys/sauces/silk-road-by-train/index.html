Silk Road by train

A rail journey from Persian walnut sauce to the noodle shops of eastern China.

The Silk Road was a set of trade routes, not one track. Walnut, spice, wheat, and fermented soy moved along it, and the sauces at these stops still taste of that traffic.

This leg is by train: slow enough to taste the change from pomegranate and walnut to soy, chile bean paste, and scallion oil.

Stops

  1. Fesenjan · Iran — Walnut and pomegranate molasses: earthy, sour, and slightly sweet.
  2. Afghan Cilantro Chutney · Afghanistan — Cilantro, chile, garlic, and vinegar: bright, hot, and sharp.
  3. Dumpling Dipping Sauce · China — Soy, black vinegar, garlic, and chile oil: salty, sharp, and hot.
  4. Hoisin Sauce · China — Fermented soybean, sugar, vinegar, and five-spice: sweet, salty, and aromatic.
  5. Scallion Oil · China — Slow-fried scallion in oil, with soy at the table: sweet, savory, and fragrant.

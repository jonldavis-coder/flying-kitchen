Caribbean by boat

Cassava bread, johnny cakes, and plantain, moving the way these coasts actually met: by sea.

The boat is not a costume. Cassava bread, bammy, and johnny cakes traveled with sailors, enslaved people, and later shop cooks.

Bitter cassava is dangerous when it is not processed. The recipes use a commercial product and say so.

Stops

  1. Cuban torrejas · Cuba — Day-old bread soaked in egg and milk, fried, and finished with cinnamon syrup and a splash of wine.
  2. Jamaican bammy · Jamaica — A dense, mild cassava round, dry and toasted on the outside, then fried so the edge crisps.
  3. Dominican casabe · Dominican Republic — A thin, dry cassava flatbread, lightly salty, crisp at the edge and bland in a way that suits a stew.
  4. Dominican yaniqueques · Dominican Republic — Small wheat rounds, salty and toasted, cooked in a slick of oil until both sides spot.
  5. Bahamian johnnycake · Bahamas — A mildly sweet wheat cake, buttery, cooked covered in a skillet so the center steams.
  6. Puerto Rican almojábanas · Puerto Rico — Fried rice-flour and cheese fritters, crisp at the edge, salty and chewy inside.
  7. Trinidadian dhalpuri roti · Trinidad and Tobago — A soft wheat roti with a layer of seasoned ground split peas that flakes when the bread is clapped.

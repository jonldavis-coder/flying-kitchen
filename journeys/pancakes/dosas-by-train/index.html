Flying Pancakes · Train

Dosas by train

A rail journey through rice, urad, and gram-flour batters, from a plain dosa to a Bengali patishapta.

South Indian fermented batters and North Indian cheelas share a griddle and not a recipe. The train is how a cook actually moves between those kitchens today.

Nothing here is a single ancient mother batter. The pages keep the ferments separate.

Stops

  1. Plain dosa · India — Crisp at the edge, faintly sour, with a toasted rice smell and a soft underside.
  2. Masala dosa · India — A fermented rice-and-urad sheet wrapped around a turmeric potato filling, with curry leaf and green chile.
  3. Rava dosa · India — A lacy, cracker-crisp semolina sheet scented with cumin, black pepper, and curry leaf.
  4. Neer dosa · India — A soft, pale, almost translucent rice roll with a clean cereal taste and no sour ferment.
  5. Uttapam · India — A thick, soft fermented cake topped with onion, tomato, and green chile.
  6. Pesarattu · India — A green-gold moong dal pancake, herbal from ginger and green chile, with a soft crisp edge.
  7. Adai · India — A sturdy mixed-lentil pancake, peppery and hot from dried chile, with curry leaf in the batter.
  8. Appam · India — A bowl-shaped rice hopper with a lacy crisp rim, a spongy sweet center, and coconut in the batter.
  9. Besan cheela · India — A savory gram-flour pancake with onion, ajwain, and cumin, soft in the middle and spotted brown.
  10. Patishapta · India — A soft rice-and-wheat crepe rolled around a coconut and jaggery filling, fragrant with cardamom.

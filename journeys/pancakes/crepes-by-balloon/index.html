Crepes by balloon

A low, slow pass over Brittany and the north of France, from wheat crepes to buckwheat galettes.

A balloon is the right speed for this coast. The batter changes faster than the landscape: white flour and milk, then buckwheat, ham, and egg.

Chandeleur, not Mardi Gras, is the French crepe day. The pages say so.

Stops

  1. Breton crepes · France — Thin wheat crepes from Brittany, butter and sugar, the cake of Chandeleur.
  2. Buckwheat galette · France — A savory Breton buckwheat crepe, lacy and a little bitter, cooked on a billig.
  3. Galette complète · France — A buckwheat galette with ham, cheese, and an egg cooked in the middle.
  4. Galette-saucisse · France — A pork sausage rolled in a buckwheat galette, the Rennes street snack.
  5. Ficelle picarde · France — A Picardy crepe rolled around ham and mushrooms in cream, then baked with cheese.
  6. Normandy apple crepes · France — Wheat crepes filled with apples cooked in butter, a Normandy dessert.
  7. Crêpes Suzette · France — Thin crepes in orange butter, with a warm orange sauce.
  8. Socca · France — A crisp-edged chickpea flatbread from Nice, eaten hot with black pepper.

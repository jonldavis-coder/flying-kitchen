Africa coast to coast

From a Moroccan baghrir to a South African pannekoek, with fermented batters in between.

The coasts are not one cuisine. Baghrir, masa, injera, canjeero, and pannekoek do not descend from each other. The plane is only a way to taste them in one reading.

Where a cake is tied to a trade in grain, the page says which grain.

Stops

  1. Moroccan baghrir · Morocco — Spongy semolina cakes, yeasted and sour-sweet, soaked with butter and honey.
  2. Nigerian masa · Nigeria — Mildly sour rice cakes with crisp rims and a soft, slightly sweet center.
  3. Ghanaian tatale · Ghana — Sweet ripe plantain fritters with ginger and a mild chile warmth, fried until the edges crisp.
  4. Injera · Ethiopia — Sour, spongy teff bread with an earthy crumb and open holes on top.
  5. Somali canjeero · Somalia — Soft, lightly sour wheat cakes full of small holes, mildly sweet at the edge.
  6. Kenyan vitumbua · Kenya — Yeasted rice cakes scented with coconut and cardamom, crisp on the curve and soft inside.
  7. Mofo gasy · Madagascar — Small, sweet rice cakes with a yeasted crumb and a browned top.
  8. South African pannekoek · South Africa — Thin wheat pancakes rolled around cinnamon sugar, with a buttery edge.

Codfish cakes for a kite day

A home version of Bermuda’s Good Friday codfish cake: soaked salt cod, potato, onion, parsley, and thyme, fried. Recipes on the island vary.

30 min hands-on, plus soaking · about 20 min frying · 8 small cakes

Tradition: Bermuda Good Friday kites

Ingredients

  • 250 g salt cod, soaked in cold water in the fridge for 12 to 24 hours, water changed two or three times
  • 400 g floury potatoes, peeled and cut into chunks
  • 1 small onion, finely chopped
  • 2 tablespoons chopped parsley
  • 1 tablespoon fresh thyme leaves
  • 1 egg
  • Black pepper
  • Plain flour, for dusting
  • Oil, for shallow frying

Steps

  1. Drain the soaked cod. Simmer it in fresh water until it flakes, about 10 minutes. Boil the potatoes until tender. Drain both.
  2. Flake the cod and pick out bones and skin. Mash it with the potatoes. Stir in the onion, parsley, thyme, egg, and pepper. The salt cod is already salty, so taste before adding any salt.
  3. Shape eight small cakes with floured hands. Dust them with flour.
  4. Fry in a shallow layer of oil over medium heat until deep gold on both sides. Drain on a rack.
  5. Eat warm, on a kite field, with a hot cross bun if you want the Bermudian pairing. Go To Bermuda describes the cake sitting in the bun.

This is a home adaptation for a picnic, not an official Bermudian standard. The Bermudian magazine says the fish-to-potato ratio is a household argument, and that thyme is the herb people insist on.

Keep fried cakes refrigerated and eat them the same day. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

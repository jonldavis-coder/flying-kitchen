Hot cross buns

A home batch of hot cross buns for Good Friday: an enriched raisin dough, a flour cross, and a light sugar glaze.

25 min, plus rising · 16 to 18 min · 8 buns

Tradition: Bermuda Good Friday kites

Ingredients

  • 300 g plain flour, plus 2 tablespoons for the cross
  • 7 g instant yeast
  • 30 g sugar
  • 1/2 teaspoon fine salt
  • 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon
  • 1/4 teaspoon ground allspice
  • 150 ml warm milk
  • 40 g unsalted butter, softened
  • 1 egg
  • 80 g raisins
  • 1 tablespoon sugar and 1 tablespoon water, for the glaze

Steps

  1. Stir the flour, yeast, sugar, salt, and spices. Add the milk, butter, and egg. Knead until the dough is smooth and springy, then knead in the raisins.
  2. Cover and leave in a warm place until about doubled, usually 60 to 75 minutes.
  3. Divide into eight pieces. Roll them smooth and set them on a lined tray with a little space between. Cover and rise until puffy, about 30 minutes. Heat the oven to 200°C.
  4. Mix the extra flour with just enough water to make a thick paste. Pipe or spoon a cross on each bun.
  5. Bake 16 to 18 minutes, until the bottoms are brown. Warm the sugar and water and brush it on while the buns are hot.
  6. For a Bermudian-style icing cross, skip the flour paste and, once the buns are cool, pipe a cross of icing sugar loosened with a few drops of water. Bermuda.com describes that finish.

The quantities are a KiteKitchen home batch. They are not copied from a bakery formula.

On a Bermuda Good Friday the bun is often the bread around a codfish cake. That pairing is the tradition. The bun alone is the British Easter bread it came from.

A shortened fiambre

A small cold salad in the spirit of Guatemalan fiambre for 1 November. A family fiambre uses many more ingredients.

40 min · 15 min for the vegetables and eggs · 6 as a salad

Tradition: Giant kites in Sumpango and Santiago Sacatepéquez

Ingredients

  • 200 g cooked chicken, sliced
  • 100 g cooked ham or a mild cooked sausage, sliced
  • 80 g cheese, sliced
  • 2 hard-boiled eggs, cut in wedges
  • A handful of cooked green beans
  • 1 cooked carrot, sliced
  • 1 small cooked beet, sliced, if you want the redder version
  • Lettuce leaves, a few olives, and pickled onion or gherkin
  • Dressing: 3 tablespoons olive oil, 1 tablespoon white vinegar, 1/2 teaspoon dried oregano, salt, and pepper

Steps

  1. Cook the vegetables and eggs. Cool them completely. Fiambre is eaten cold.
  2. Whisk the dressing.
  3. Line a wide dish with lettuce. Arrange the chicken, ham, cheese, eggs, vegetables, olives, and pickles in groups so people can see the parts. Spoon the dressing over.
  4. Chill at least an hour. Carry it cold. Do not leave it in the sun.

INGUAT describes fiambre as a wide cold salad of meats, cheeses, and vegetables. Families use far more ingredients than this list. Red and white versions differ, often by the beet. This is a shortened picnic salad, not a complete family recipe.

Leaving out the chicken and ham makes a meat-free plate. That is a KiteKitchen option, not the classic dish.

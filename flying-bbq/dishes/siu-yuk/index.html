Cantonese Siu Yuk

Roast pork belly with blistered skin, salt, and five spice under the crackling.

History

Siu yuk is Cantonese roast pork with a blistered skin, sold in the same roast shops as char siu. The skin is dried, salted, and hit with high heat so it bubbles, while five spice seasons the meat underneath. A restaurant oven runs hotter and drier than most homes. This is a home-oven version, labeled as such; the belly is a whole cut and is rested; there is no soy sauce in this rub, so the written recipe has no wheat.

Cultural background

Cantonese roast shops hang or rack the belly until the skin crackles. Home cooks can dry the skin and use a hot oven. There is pork, salt, and five spice. There is no dairy and no wheat in this rub.

Why it tastes this way

Dry skin plus high heat makes the crackling, and the fat underneath bastes the meat. Five spice stays under the skin so it does not burn. Salt is the main seasoning, which is why siu yuk tastes less sweet than char siu from the same shop. A covered braise would soften the skin and miss the dish.

How it is made

Salt and dry a pork belly, rub five spice on the meat side, and roast it in a home oven until the center is done and the skin has blistered.

Authentic touch

Dry the skin, uncovered, in the refrigerator before you roast it. Wet skin steams and stays leathery.

How it is used

Chop it into pieces and eat it with rice and mustard greens.

Field notes

  • Siu yuk is Cantonese roast pork bought for the crackling skin.
  • This rub has no soy sauce, so the written recipe has no wheat.

Ingredients

  • 2.5 pounds skin-on pork belly, in one slab
  • 2 teaspoons salt
  • 1 teaspoon five-spice powder
  • 1 teaspoon white vinegar
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt, extra, for the skin

Steps

  1. Score the skin in a tight crosshatch, cutting into the skin and not deep into the meat. Rub vinegar and the extra salt on the skin. Rub five spice and the remaining salt on the meat side only.
  2. Refrigerate the slab uncovered for at least 4 hours so the skin dries. The pork is still raw. Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  3. Heat a home oven to 450°F (230°C). Set the belly on a rack, skin side up. This is a home oven, not a roast-shop oven.
  4. Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  5. Roast until the center reaches at least 145°F (63°C) and the skin has blistered. If the meat is ready and the skin is pale, give it a short hotter blast and check the temperature again. Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  6. Rest the pork 3 minutes, then chop it. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.

Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. A shoulder you want to pull can continue toward 195°F (90°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A final few minutes of very high heat helps the bubbles if the skin is already dry.
  • A spoon of vinegar rubbed on the skin is used by some cooks to help it dry.

Pairings

  • rice
  • mustard greens
  • cucumber

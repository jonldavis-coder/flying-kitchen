Saarland Schwenker

Pork steak with onion, paprika, and wood smoke from a swinging grill.

History

Schwenker is both the swinging grill of Saarland and the pork steak cooked on it. The grate hangs from a tripod and is swung over a wood fire, which is how the name, from the verb to swing, got attached to the meat. It is a regional outdoor meal, often with bread and salad. The steak is a whole cut, seasoned simply.

Cultural background

Saarland households and clubs cook schwenker when there is room for the tripod. It is not a sausage and not a Berlin currywurst. A kettle grill can stand in, but the swing is the local method.

Why it tastes this way

A thick pork steak from the neck or shoulder stays juicy over a wood fire if the heat is high and the cook keeps it moving. Onion and paprika in the marinade season the surface. The swinging grate lets the cook dodge flare-ups from the fat, which is why the tool and the taste belong together.

How it is made

Marinate pork neck steaks briefly, then cook them on a swinging grill or a hot grate over wood until they reach a safe temperature and rest.

Authentic touch

Cut the steaks thick and swing or move them when the fat flares. Do not press the juice out with a spatula.

How it is used

With bread, raw onion, and a green salad. Beer is a common drink beside it, not an ingredient in the meat.

Field notes

  • In Saarland the word schwenker names both the swinging grill and the pork steak.
  • The grate hangs over a wood fire and is swung aside when the flames rise.

Ingredients

  • 6 pork neck steaks, about 1 inch thick
  • 1 onion, sliced
  • 1 tablespoon sweet paprika
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 2 tablespoons oil

Steps

  1. Mix the onion, paprika, salt, and oil. Turn the steaks in the mixture and rest them while the fire burns down to coals.
  2. Cook on a swinging grill or a hot grate over wood, turning as needed so the fat does not flame up.
  3. Cook whole pork to at least 145°F (63°C) and give it a 3-minute rest.
  4. Serve with bread and onion. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. A shoulder you want to pull can continue toward 195°F (90°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A standard grill if you have no tripod, still over wood or charcoal.
  • A shorter marinade of only salt, pepper, and onion.

Pairings

  • Bread
  • Raw onion
  • Green salad

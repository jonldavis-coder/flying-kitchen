Vermont Maple-Cider Chicken

Grilled chicken with a light maple and apple-cider glaze.

  • 15 min prep
  • 50 min cook
  • Serves 4

History

Maple sugaring is a northeastern Indigenous practice, including among the Abenaki and other nations of this region. The glaze on this chicken is a later home cook, not a ceremony and not a sugaring method. It uses a little pure maple syrup and fresh apple cider on a backyard bird. Maple syrup is not honey.

Cultural background

A home grill chicken with cider and maple. It does not describe Indigenous sugaring, and it does not use honey.

Why it tastes this way

Maple is a tree sugar with a drier sweetness than honey, and cider adds acid so the glaze does not taste only sweet. On chicken skin, a late brush caramelizes without a long smoke. The Vermont pantry is why these two ingredients meet on a weeknight grill.

How it is made

Grill chicken and brush on a reduced cider and maple glaze near the end.

Authentic touch

Glaze late. Maple syrup burns if it sits on a hot grate for the whole cook.

How it is used

Jointed chicken with the thin glaze. It does not need a honey butter, and none is used.

Field notes

  • Abenaki and other northeastern Indigenous peoples sugared maple; this glaze is a later home cook, not a ceremony.
  • Pure maple syrup is not honey, and this recipe does not use honey.

Ingredients

  • 1 chicken , halved, about 4 pounds
  • 1 teaspoon kosher salt
  • 1/2 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1 cup fresh nonalcoholic apple cider
  • 2 tablespoons pure maple syrup

Steps

  1. Salt and pepper the chicken. Simmer the cider and maple syrup until reduced by about half. This glaze uses maple, not honey.
  2. Heat a grill with a cooler zone.
  3. Grill the chicken until the thickest part of the thigh, not touching bone, reaches 165°F (74°C).
  4. Brush with glaze in the last few minutes over the cooler side. Rest, then serve. Do not serve it rare.

Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) in the thickest part, not touching bone.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Half chickens are easier to grill evenly than small pieces that overcook.

Pairings

  • roasted squash
  • green salad
  • bread

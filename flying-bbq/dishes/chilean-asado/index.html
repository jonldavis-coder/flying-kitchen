Chilean Asado

Origin: Chile · 38° S, 71° W

Next stop: Brazilian Picanha · on Asado and churrasco by plane, by Plane

Pronounced ah-SAH-doh, mer-KEN

Salted beef from a Chilean grill, with a little merkén and a fresh relish beside it.

History

A Chilean asado is beef grilled in a backyard quincho, over wood or charcoal, salted and eaten with pebre more often than with Argentine chimichurri. Merkén, a smoked chile and coriander condiment from Mapuche cooking, is one seasoning Chilean cooks reach for. The asado itself is a criollo grill, not a claim that the whole fire is a Mapuche ceremony. This page is that Chilean grill.

Cultural background

Pebre, a chopped onion, tomato, and cilantro relish, is the usual table sauce and is listed as a pairing rather than cooked onto the meat. The beef is a whole cut. If you cannot buy merkén, say that smoked paprika is not merkén.

Why it tastes this way

Coals brown the beef, and salt keeps the flavor on the meat. Merkén adds smoke and a mild chile heat that a plain Argentine salt crust does not have. The fresh pebre at the table is sharp and raw, so the plate tastes of grill plus a salad, not of a cooked sauce.

How it is made

Salt a steak, dust it with merkén, and grill it over coals to a whole-beef temperature.

Authentic touch

Use merkén as a finish or a light rub, not a thick paste. The beef should still taste like beef.

How it is used

Rest it, slice it, and eat it with pebre, bread, and a tomato salad. Bread is a pairing and adds wheat.

Field notes

  • A Chilean asado is its own grill, often served with pebre rather than Argentine chimichurri.
  • Merkén is a Mapuche smoked-chile and coriander condiment.

Ingredients

  • 2 pounds beef steak or a small roast
  • 1 tablespoon coarse salt
  • 1 teaspoon merkén, or 1 teaspoon smoked paprika labeled as not merkén
  • 1 tablespoon neutral oil
  • Wood or charcoal burned to coals

Steps

  1. Oil the beef and season it with salt and merkén. It is still raw and is not ready to eat. Whole cuts are not ready to eat until they reach at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  2. Burn the fuel to coals and set a grate over them. Whole cuts are not ready to eat until they reach at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes.
  3. Grill until the thickest part reads 145°F (63°C). Rest it 3 minutes.
  4. Slice the rested beef. It should have reached 145°F (63°C) before the rest. Serve pebre on the side. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.

Cook whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, or goat to at least 145°F (63°C) and rest 3 minutes. A shoulder you want to pull can continue toward 195°F (90°C).

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • No merkén: grill the steak with salt only, and do not rename smoked paprika.
  • A thicker roast on the same fire still needs 145°F (63°C) and a 3-minute rest.

Pairings

  • pebre
  • bread
  • tomato salad

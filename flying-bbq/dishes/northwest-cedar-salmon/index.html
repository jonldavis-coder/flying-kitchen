Northwest Cedar-Plank Salmon

Salmon with a light cedar smell, salt, and a moist flake.

  • 1 hr 10 min prep
  • 20 min cook
  • Serves 4
  • about 4 servings

History

Northwest Coast practices differ by nation, including Coast Salish, Nuu-chah-nulth, Tlingit, Haida, and others. Salmon is central to many of those foodways, and the methods are not one recipe. This is a home plank grill, not a potlatch dish. The country of the recipe as written is the United States, and salmon cookery of this coast is also Canadian.

Cultural background

A home cedar-plank salmon. It is not a potlatch dish and not a single nation's recipe. Soak an untreated plank.

Why it tastes this way

The wet plank steams the salmon a little while the cedar perfumes the surface, and a hot grill underneath finishes it. Salt is enough because the fish is already rich. The plank is a backyard technique, which is why it should not be described as a ceremonial cook.

How it is made

Grill a salted salmon fillet on a soaked cedar plank until it flakes.

Authentic touch

Use an untreated cedar plank and soak it. A varnished board is not a cooking tool.

How it is used

Lifted off the plank in portions, with lemon. Do not eat the plank.

Field notes

  • Coast Salish, Nuu-chah-nulth, Tlingit, Haida, and other Northwest Coast nations do not share one salmon recipe.
  • A plank on a home grill is not a potlatch dish.

Ingredients

  • 1 untreated cedar plank, soaked in water for at least 1 hour
  • 1 1/2 pounds salmon fillet
  • 1 teaspoon kosher salt
  • 1/2 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1 lemon

Steps

  1. Soak the untreated plank. Do not use a finished or painted board. This is a home grill, not a potlatch.
  2. Salt and pepper the salmon. Heat a grill to medium-high and set the soaked plank on it until it begins to smell aromatic, not until it is in flames.
  3. Lay the salmon on the plank, cover the grill if you can, and cook until the thickest part reaches 145°F (63°C) and the fish flakes and looks opaque.
  4. Serve off the plank with lemon. Discard the plank if it has charred through.

Cook fish to 145°F (63°C), until it is opaque and flakes.

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 to 4 days, or freeze. Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C). This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A covered grill helps the top of a thick fillet cook before the plank scorches.

Pairings

  • lemon
  • green salad
  • boiled potatoes

History

Pol sambol is the Sri Lankan coconut relish of scraped coconut, red onion, chile, lime, and often Maldive fish flakes. This version leaves out the dried fish so the relish is plant-based, and the page says so. The classic with Maldive fish is saltier and more savory.

Cultural background

It is on the table with rice, hoppers, and curry. Red onion and lime are as important as the coconut. Chile is not shy.

Why it tastes this way

Fresh coconut, onion, lime, and chile taste sharp and hot. This version leaves out Maldive fish, so it is less savory than the classic with dried fish. Lime and onion are as important as the coconut.

How it is made

Coconut is mixed by hand with onion, chile, lime, and salt until it looks moist and red.

Authentic touch

Mix and squeeze it by hand so the coconut turns moist and red. Serve it the same day.

How it is used

Serve it with rice or hoppers.

Field notes

  • Pol is the Sinhala word for coconut, which is the body of the relish.
  • The fish-free version is a real household choice and is milder in savor than the Maldive-fish one.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups freshly grated coconut
  • 1 small red onion, minced
  • 1 teaspoon chili flakes
  • 2 tablespoons lime juice
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt

Steps

  1. Mix everything with your hands, squeezing so the coconut turns moist.
  2. Taste for salt and lime.
  3. It should be loose, not a dry sprinkle.
  4. Serve the same day.
  5. Eat it with rice.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Maldive fish flakes are the savory classic and add fish.
  • A milder chile makes it a side rather than a hot relish.

Pairings

  • rice
  • hoppers
  • dal

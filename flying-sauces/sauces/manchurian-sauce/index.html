History

Manchurian sauce is an Indian Chinese restaurant sauce, not a sauce from Manchuria. It was developed in Kolkata’s Chinese community cooking in the twentieth century, often credited to kitchens serving Indo-Chinese food, and popularized as chicken or gobi Manchurian. The sauce is soy, garlic, ginger, chile, and a cornstarch gloss.

Cultural background

It is poured over fried cauliflower or chicken. The name is a restaurant flourish. The flavor is Indian Chinese: hotter and more garlicky than many Cantonese brown sauces.

Why it tastes this way

Garlic, ginger, soy, and chile taste savory and hot, glossed with starch. It is an Indian Chinese restaurant sauce. The name is not a Manchu recipe. Ketchup, when used, adds sweetness.

How it is made

Garlic, ginger, and chile fry, soy and stock are added, and a cornstarch slurry glosses the sauce.

Authentic touch

Fry the garlic and ginger hard, then thicken the sauce and toss it with food that is already fried.

How it is used

Toss it with fried cauliflower or chicken and serve with fried rice or noodles.

Field notes

  • The sauce is Indian Chinese. It is not a historic Manchu recipe.
  • Cornstarch gives it the glossy coat associated with the dish.

Ingredients

  • 1 tablespoon oil
  • 4 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 tablespoon ginger
  • 1 green chile, minced
  • 2 tablespoons soy sauce
  • 1 tablespoon ketchup
  • 1 cup water
  • 1 teaspoon sugar
  • 1 teaspoon cornstarch mixed with 2 tablespoons water

Steps

  1. Fry the garlic, ginger, and chile in the oil.
  2. Add the soy, ketchup, water, and sugar and simmer 5 minutes.
  3. Stir in the cornstarch slurry and cook until glossy.
  4. Toss with fried cauliflower or chicken.
  5. Serve at once.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A hotter chile is the street version.
  • Ketchup in the sauce is common in many Indian Chinese kitchens.

Pairings

  • fried cauliflower
  • fried rice
  • noodles

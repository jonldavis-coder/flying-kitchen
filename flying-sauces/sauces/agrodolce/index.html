Agrodolce

Vinegar and sugar, with onion or fruit: sweet, sour, and sticky.

History

Agrodolce is the sweet-sour sauce of Sicily, a tradition shaped by the island’s Arab and Mediterranean cooking. Vinegar and sugar, often with onion, mint, or dried fruit, glaze vegetables and meats. It is a method as much as one recipe. Cipolline in agrodolce are the famous small onions.

Cultural background

Sicilian cooking puts agrodolce on onions, pumpkin, eggplant, and sometimes fish. The sauce should be both sweet and clearly sour, not a mild glaze.

Why it tastes this way

Vinegar and sugar, reduced, taste sweet and sour at once. Sicilian cooking uses that glaze on onions and vegetables. If either side disappears, it is no longer agrodolce.

How it is made

Sugar and vinegar reduce with onion until syrupy, and mint or raisins can be added at the end.

Authentic touch

Reduce until syrupy and taste both the sour and the sweet. Add vinegar back if the sugar wins.

How it is used

Toss it with small onions, or spoon it over roasted vegetables and grilled meat.

Field notes

  • Agrodolce means sour-sweet, and both sides should stay obvious.
  • Sicily’s version is part of a wider Mediterranean sweet-sour family, including gastriques.

Ingredients

  • 1 tablespoon olive oil
  • 1 onion , thinly sliced
  • 2 tablespoons sugar
  • 1/4 cup red wine vinegar
  • 1/4 cup water
  • 1 teaspoon chopped mint
  • Salt

Steps

  1. Soften the onion in the oil.
  2. Add the sugar, vinegar, and water.
  3. Simmer until the onion is glazed and the liquid is syrupy.
  4. Stir in the mint and salt.
  5. Serve warm or at room temperature.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Raisins and pine nuts are a Sicilian sweet variation and add a tree-nut allergen.
  • A spoon of honey instead of sugar is common and is not vegan.

Pairings

  • cipolline onions
  • roasted pumpkin
  • grilled pork

Currywurst Sauce

History

Currywurst sauce is the spiced ketchup served over sliced fried sausage in Germany, dusted again with curry powder. The dish is associated with the postwar years, and Herta Heuwer in Berlin is often credited with selling it in 1949. That attribution is widely repeated. The sauce itself is a thickened, curried tomato ketchup, not an Indian curry.

Cultural background

It is street food in Berlin and the Ruhr, eaten with a fork and a roll or fries. The sausage is cut into pieces and buried under the sauce.

Why it tastes this way

Ketchup simmered with curry powder tastes sweet, tangy, and spiced. A second dusting of curry on the sausage is why it tastes more of curry than the sauce alone. It is not an Indian curry.

How it is made

Ketchup, curry powder, onion, and vinegar simmer until slightly thick, then more curry powder is sprinkled on the sausage at serving.

Authentic touch

Simmer the sauce, then dust the finished sausage with more curry powder. Both steps are the dish.

How it is used

Pour it over sliced fried bratwurst and dust with curry powder.

Field notes

  • The sauce is curried ketchup, which is why a second dusting of curry powder goes on top.
  • Berlin and the Ruhr both claim currywurst culture.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup ketchup
  • 1 tablespoon curry powder, plus more to serve
  • 1 teaspoon paprika
  • 1 teaspoon white vinegar
  • 1 teaspoon sugar
  • 1/4 cup water
  • Pinch of chili powder

Steps

  1. Simmer the ketchup, curry powder, paprika, vinegar, sugar, water, and chili 10 minutes.
  2. The sauce should be pourable.
  3. Slice a fried sausage.
  4. Pour the sauce over it.
  5. Dust with more curry powder.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A smoother sauce is blended.
  • Worcestershire is used in some stands and adds anchovy.

Pairings

  • bratwurst
  • fries
  • bread roll

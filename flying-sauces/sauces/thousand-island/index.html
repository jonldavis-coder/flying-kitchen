History

Thousand Island dressing takes its name from the Thousand Islands of the St. Lawrence River. Several early-1900s origin stories circulate, so this page does not pick one inventor. The dressing is mayonnaise with ketchup or chili sauce and chopped pickle.

Cultural background

It is a salad dressing and a sandwich spread. Relish is the chunky sweet-sour note. Russian dressing is a close cousin, often sharper and without as much relish.

Why it tastes this way

Mayonnaise is the body. Ketchup sweetens it. Pickle relish adds acid and crunch. That trio tastes mild and sweet, which is why it works on cold seafood and salads.

How it is made

Mayonnaise, ketchup, and relish are stirred together.

Authentic touch

Chop or use a fine relish and chill the dressing. Taste it. It should be more creamy than ketchup-sweet.

How it is used

Use it on wedges, seafood salads, or sandwiches.

Field notes

  • The name is the river region. The inventor stories disagree.
  • Relish is what makes it different from a plain pink mayo.

Advertisement

Ingredients

  • 1 cup mayonnaise
  • 1/4 cup ketchup
  • 2 tablespoons pickle relish
  • 1 teaspoon vinegar
  • Pinch of salt
  • Pinch of black pepper

Steps

  1. Stir everything together.
  2. Chill 20 minutes.
  3. Taste for salt.
  4. Serve on salad or sandwiches.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Russian dressing is sharper, often with horseradish or more spice.
  • A chopped egg is an older addition.

Pairings

  • lettuce
  • shrimp salad
  • sandwiches

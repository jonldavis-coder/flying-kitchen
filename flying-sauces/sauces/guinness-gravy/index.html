Guinness Gravy

History

Guinness has been brewed at St. James’s Gate in Dublin since 1759. Cooking onions and stock with that stout is ordinary Irish pub and home gravy for beef, sausages, and pies. There is no single inventor and no founding year for the gravy. The stout is the Irish ingredient. The method is an onion gravy.

Cultural background

A roast or a pan of sausages leaves fat and browned bits. The stout replaces some of the liquid a French gravy would take from wine. It is pub food, served in a jug, not a glazed restaurant sauce.

Why it tastes this way

Roasted onion is sweet. Stout is bitter and malty, which is why the gravy is not a plain brown sauce. Flour keeps it thick enough to cling to meat, and a long simmer cooks off the raw beer taste. Beef stock, when you use it, is the savory body under the malt.

How it is made

Onion cooks in butter, flour makes a roux, and stout and stock simmer into a gravy.

Authentic touch

Boil the stout for a few minutes before you call it done. If it still tastes like a fresh pint, it needs more time. A vegetable stock makes a meatless gravy, and it will taste lighter.

How it is used

Serve it with roast beef, sausages, mashed potatoes, or a pie.

Field notes

  • The brewery date is 1759. The gravy has no matching founding date.
  • Stout is bitter. Sugar is not what makes this gravy taste like itself.

Ingredients

  • 2 tablespoons butter
  • 1 large onion, sliced
  • 2 tablespoons flour
  • 1 cup Guinness or another dry Irish stout
  • 1 cup beef stock
  • Salt and black pepper

Steps

  1. Cook the onion in the butter until it is soft and browned at the edges.
  2. Stir in the flour and cook 1 minute.
  3. Pour in the stout and whisk until smooth.
  4. Add the stock and simmer 15 minutes, until the raw beer taste is gone.
  5. Season with salt and pepper.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Vegetable stock in place of beef stock keeps it free of meat.
  • A spoon of mustard sharpens it. That is optional.

Pairings

  • roast beef
  • sausages
  • mashed potatoes
  • pie

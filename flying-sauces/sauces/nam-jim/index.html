Nam Jim Seafood

Lime, fish sauce, garlic, and chile, with a little sugar: sharp and hot.

  • 10 min prep
  • 0 min cook
  • Serves 4
  • about 3/4 cup

History

Nam jim is the family name for Thai dipping sauces. The seafood nam jim is garlic, chile, lime, fish sauce, and a little sugar, served with grilled prawns and fish. It is close to nuoc cham and not the same balance. Palm sugar is the usual sweetener when it is used.

Cultural background

Grilled seafood stalls set this sauce beside the charcoal. The garlic is pounded, not left in slices only.

Why it tastes this way

Pound garlic and chile, then lime and fish sauce taste sharper than a sweet chile dip. A little sugar rounds it for grilled seafood, which is mild and smoky.

How it is made

Garlic and chile are pounded, then lime, fish sauce, and a little sugar are stirred in.

Authentic touch

Pound the garlic. Sliced garlic alone will not perfume the sauce the same way.

How it is used

Serve it with grilled prawns or whole fish.

Field notes

  • Nam jim means dipping sauce. The seafood version is the garlicky one.
  • It should taste loud, because the grilled seafood is mild.

Advertisement

Ingredients

  • 3 garlic cloves
  • 4 bird’s eye chiles
  • 3 tablespoons lime juice
  • 2 tablespoons fish sauce
  • 1 teaspoon sugar

Steps

  1. Pound the garlic and chiles.
  2. Stir in the lime, fish sauce, and sugar.
  3. Taste for a balance of sour, salt, and heat.
  4. Serve with grilled seafood.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Nam jim jaew is the roasted-chile Isan sauce and a different recipe.
  • More sugar makes it closer to a sweet chile dip.

Pairings

  • grilled prawns
  • grilled squid
  • green mango

Who settled the flavor

Appalachia

Scots-Irish, German, and English settlement, beside Native and Black mountain cooking.

Scots-Irish, English, and German settlers farmed the Appalachian valleys in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries. Red-eye gravy, from country ham and black coffee, and sawmill gravy, the sausage and milk gravy, are that mountain and upland table. Tomato gravy and sorghum glaze belong with them: pantry cooking, sour and sweet, meant for biscuits and ham.

Cherokee and other Native nations of these mountains grew corn and foraged long before those settlements. This chapter does not assign chocolate gravy, chow-chow, or sorghum glaze to them. Those recipes are later mountain home cooking. No Cherokee sauce is invented here to fill a gap.

African American families have lived and cooked in these mountains for generations, including under slavery and after it. Chow-chow, the mustard-and-turmeric vegetable pickle, is a Southern and Appalachian relish with a cloudy name history. The page for it does not pretend the name has one proven origin. Chocolate gravy is cocoa, flour, milk, and sugar for breakfast biscuits. It is a gravy, not a ceremonial Native dish.
